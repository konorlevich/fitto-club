# FITTO CLUB: visual references and direction concepts

**How I checked the links.** I opened every URL below with WebFetch on 2026-09-27. WebFetch only returns page text, not rendered screenshots. So "loads" means the page answered with real content. Some of the visual notes (layout, type, colour) come from my own knowledge of these brands and the case-study write-ups, not from pixels I saw. Look at each one in a browser before you pin it to a moodboard.

**1. Strava.** Its orange (#FC4C02) is almost the same as FITTO's #FF4401. It is the best live example of a small, disciplined orange system on a large product.
- **Borrow:** orange as a signal, not wallpaper. Use it for one action, one number and one highlight per screen, and keep everything else neutral. Keep the copy plain and a little wry: headings like "Start by sweating" and "Get *better* by analysis" use one italic word for tone.
- **Don't borrow:** the app-marketing layout (three-column feature blocks, sign-up hero) or the white SaaS look.

**2. Tracksmith.** A running brand built on collegiate heritage, the "amateur spirit" and editorial pacing. Its hare mark is used like a school crest.
- **Borrow:** the varsity and collegiate cues (crests, stripes, letterman details) used with restraint and good typography. Section headings with one emphasised word ("*Back*", "*Here*"). Pages that read like a magazine: large editorial image, short text, then product. The mascot works as an emblem, not a cartoon.
- **Don't borrow:** the muted New England palette, the prep-school price feel, or the slow luxury pacing. FITTO is a friendly neighbourhood gym.

**3. Duolingo, the Duo mascot system.** The benchmark for a mascot used as a system, not a sticker. Duo is built from one or two basic shapes, has rounded edges and no sharp points, and comes with a fixed set of expressions. He shows up in the awkward moments (errors, empty states, reminders) to make them funny instead of annoying.
- **Borrow for the panda:**
  - Write a short rulebook.
  - Draw 6–8 fixed poses:
    - hi
    - thumbs up
    - sweating on the sled
    - whistle (coach)
    - asleep on a massage table
    - confused (404)
    - flexing (a joke, never serious)
  - Use him in the functional moments: form sent, 404, "first visit free", the language switcher.
  - Take the panda's voice from the meme reels.
- **Don't borrow:** the green, the 3D renders, or the guilt-trip tone.

**4. Mailchimp brand assets.** One mascot (Freddie, always winking), one hero colour (Cavendish yellow) and plenty of space around both.
- **Borrow:** one fixed personality trait for the panda that never changes. Clear rules for when the panda sits with the logo and when he doesn't. The idea that the brand colour can be a full background, which is how FITTO's Instagram already uses orange.
- **Don't borrow:** Mailchimp's illustration style or its Cooper-style serif.

**5. Frame (London boutique studios).** A friendly, funny, non-hardcore voice on a real gym brand: "We lift moods. And butts." It is inclusive and never intimidating. This is the closest match to FITTO's tone.
- **Borrow:** copy that jokes but still gives clear information, and uppercase punchlines kept short.
- **Don't borrow:** the "need-state" card grid, countdown timers or the app-download block. These are typical boutique-studio chain patterns.

**6. UPA! Climbing (designed by Allie Heesh and Sara Silva Santos).** A climbing gym that deliberately avoided industry clichés. It draws on zines, collage and imperfect lettering so newcomers don't feel intimidated. The live site's copy is cheeky: roughly, "because not all vices are bad".
- **Borrow:** the stated goal of being welcoming for newcomers while still credible. Take hand-made or print-culture texture from outside fitness. A sticker or collage layer (the panda, arcs, tape labels) over real photos.
- **Don't borrow:** the brush lettering or the yellow. They are a different brand.

**7. Picklewood by People People.** A sports club that parodies the country club. It has a mascot (a woodchuck, a nod to the gopher in the film *Caddyshack*), tongue-in-cheek plaid and argyle patterns, and trophy-case humour.
- **Borrow:** the parody idea. FITTO could play at being a "varsity athletic club" the same way. Use a playful pattern (FITTO's thick C and semicircle arcs) as a real system across walls, merch and web section breaks. Let the mascot appear in the physical space as well as on the site.
- **Don't borrow:** the midcentury script or the green and clay palette.

**8. Nettie Pickleball by Hoodzpah.** Retro racquet-club nostalgia, made current. The wordmark uses an arcing swash on the N that sends a ball to the dot of the i. Each paddle has a pattern with a story behind it.
- **Borrow:** use FITTO's arcs as movement arrows, for example an arc that throws the reader from one section to the next. Give each pattern variant a small story, such as one arc tile per trainer or per membership tier.
- **Don't borrow:** its fonts (New Kansas and Montserrat) or the pastel retro palette.

**9. Gymtown by Komma.** A small Dutch neighbourhood gym ("accessible sport") in orange and black. It uses Power Grotesk with Instrument Sans, and a slanted graphic device taken from a town map. The device runs across the website, signage, clothes and doors.
- **Borrow:** this is the closest real peer in size and colour. It shows that one graphic device (theirs is the map-slant, ours would be the arc) can carry the identity across both the building and the website. Pair one heavy grotesk with one plain body sans.
- **Don't borrow:** the generic corporate photography or the slant motif.

**10. Chalk Gyms (2012 identity).** This single-site gym built its whole brand on one typeface plus orange. The "C" was redrawn as a smiley face.
- **Borrow:** the formula "typeface + colour = brand", and a letter doubling as a face. FITTO's "C" arc is already a smile or panda-ear shape. Treat the arc as the brand's grin.
- **Don't borrow:** the stencil serif. Also, the current live site no longer looks like this, so don't send people there.

**11. The Gym, Vienna (fertig design).** A type-only identity for an inclusive, "non-toxic fitness" event that pushed back against typical gym body culture. It uses bouncing baselines and mixed glyph sizes across posters, flags, rugs and cushions.
- **Borrow:** this is the proof that a gym can look cheeky without bro clichés. Try bouncing or jumping letters for one or two key words, for example a jumping "FREE" in "first visit free". Use type as the main image when photos are weak.
- **Don't borrow:** the art-event styling. It is too conceptual for a membership site.

**12. Lite Brite Neon.** How real neon makers show their work: real tubes photographed on dark backgrounds, very little text, gallery restraint.
- **Borrow:** the right way to handle neon. FITTO already has real neon (the logo and the pink and purple light columns), so photograph it. Don't fake it with CSS glows everywhere. Keep the glow effect for the single logo moment.
- **Don't borrow:** the gallery coolness or the lack of personality.

**Bonus: MUNDIAL.** A football culture magazine that is witty but premium, and works as both a magazine and a shop.
- **Borrow:** writing about sport with humour and editorial confidence. It fits the trainer bios and the "About" story.

## B. Font notes (script support checked in Google Fonts metadata, 2026-09-27)

Only three Google Fonts families include Georgian:
- **Noto Sans Georgian.** Width axis 62.5–100 and weight 100–900, so a condensed Black is possible.
- **Noto Serif Georgian.**
- **Google Sans.** Also covers Cyrillic. Check its licence before relying on it.

**No free varsity or chamfered display face covers Georgian.** Plan to draw Georgian (Mtavruli) headline lockups by hand for the few big KA headings. Use a companion font for everything else.

Useful free options:
- **Tektur (OFL).** Covers Latin, Cyrillic, Greek and Vietnamese. Width axis 75–100, weight 400–900. Its chamfered, angular forms echo the FITTO letters. This is the best free display match for EN and RU.
- **Russo One (OFL).** Covers Latin and Cyrillic. Heavy and chamfered, but only one style.
- **Rubik Mono One (OFL).** Covers Latin, Cyrillic and Hebrew. Heavy and blocky, close in spirit to the Arial Black "CLUB".
- **Unbounded (OFL).** Covers Latin and Cyrillic, weight 200–900. Wide and rounded, friendlier.
- **Onest (OFL).** Covers Latin and Cyrillic, weight 100–900. A clean body sans.
- **FiraGO (OFL, bBox Type).** Covers Latin, Cyrillic, Greek and Georgian (the Georgian was designed by Akaki Razmadze with Anja Meiners). The single best free body family for all three languages.
- **TBC Contractica.** A free Georgian font from TBC Bank. Search results suggest it also has Latin and Cyrillic, but I only found it on unofficial mirrors. Get it from the official source and confirm the licence before using it.

Paid options if there's budget: Graphik Georgian (type.today) and Adapter Georgian (Rosetta). TypeTogether also has Georgian families.

## C. Three direction concepts

### 1. "FITTO Varsity" (athletic department parody)

The site plays at being the athletic department of a tiny, very friendly college.
- **Format:** black pages, orange used like ink. The outlined, chamfered FITTO letters become jersey numbers for sections (01 Memberships, 02 Trainers).
- **Trainers page:** a team roster. Each coach gets a real photo, a jersey number, the languages they speak as letterman patches (EN / RU / KA), and a one-line meme quote.
- **Memberships:** "Tryouts". The first visit is a "Walk-on pass", and the free PT session is "Coach's first practice on us".
- **The arc pattern:** chenille-patch borders and track-lane or stadium curves that divide sections. Never used as background noise.
- **The panda:** the team mascot. It appears as a crest in the footer, with the whistle pose on Trainers and asleep on the Massage page. It gets a one-liner from the Duolingo-style rulebook, used only in small, functional spots.
- **Type:**
  - Display: Tektur Black for EN/RU, set in outline (stroke only, which matches the logo) for the big numbers.
  - Short labels in Rubik Mono One, echoing "CLUB".
  - Body text in FiraGO for all three languages.
  - KA headings: hand-drawn Mtavruli lockups, or Noto Sans Georgian Black at condensed width with an outline stroke.
- **Photos:** real coaches shot with direct flash against the gym's own backdrops (plant wall, red sled track). Full colour, a little grain, no filters, no stock.
- **References:** Tracksmith, Picklewood, Nettie, Duolingo, Frame (tone).
- **Risk:** it can slide into "American college merch". Keep it witty and local, for example a "Vake Athletic Dept." line.

### 2. "Orange Room" (poster club, flipping the Instagram)

This direction brings FITTO's Instagram look to the site. Whole pages or big sections are flat #FF4401 with black type and the white panda. Black is saved for the Massage page and the footer.
- **This is the strongest way to avoid looking like other Tbilisi gyms**, which are almost all dark-hero sites.
- **Layout:** Swiss-poster rhythm. One huge headline per screen, set in heavy grotesk capitals like the "CLUB" wordmark, then short, clear text.
- **The arc pattern:** the key device. The thick C and semicircle shapes crop photos (half-moon photo windows), and they rotate as section transitions. The Chalk Gyms idea applies here too: read the C arc as the brand's grin.
- **The panda:** a flat die-cut sticker "slapped" onto photos and prices at a slight angle, with a small drop-shadow like paper, not glass.
- **Bouncing type:** borrowed from The Gym Vienna, for one or two words per page ("FREE", "FIRST", "PANDA-APPROVED").
- **Type:**
  - Display: Rubik Mono One or Russo One for EN/RU.
  - KA: Noto Sans Georgian Black, or custom lockups.
  - Body: FiraGO or Onest.
- **Photos:** real-colour photos inside arc crops, cut out on orange. The reel-style humour appears as captioned stills from the meme reels.
- **References:** Mailchimp (hero colour as background), Gymtown (orange-and-black small gym, one device throughout), Chalk Gyms, Strava (orange discipline), The Gym Vienna.
- **Risks:** WCAG contrast. Black on #FF4401 passes; white on #FF4401 fails for body text, so keep body text black on orange. There is also a risk of orange fatigue, so vary it with black and off-white pages.

### 3. "Night Shift in Vake" (restrained neon)

This is the one direction that uses neon, and it only works with strict rules.
- **The line:** the logo's tube outline becomes the site's only line language. A 2px orange stroke with rounded ends is used for:
  - dividers
  - arrows
  - underlines
  - icons, drawn only when needed, never as an icon grid
  - the outlined FITTO letters
- **The glow rule:** real glow appears once, on the logo, possibly with a single "switching on" flicker when the page loads. There is no CSS neon on body headings.
- **Colour:** comes from photography of the real interior, with the pink and purple light columns and red sled track shot at dusk like Lite Brite Neon's portfolio.
- **Massage page:** switches to the pink and purple of the light columns as a calmer sub-palette.
- **The panda:** drawn in the same single-line tube style, like a neon sign mascot. It lights up in the functional moments (form sent, 404).
- **The arc pattern:** appears as thick solid shapes only on orange "flyer" blocks (pricing, first visit free). This gives the dark pages warm moments.
- **Type:**
  - Display: Tektur (outlined, wide) for EN/RU.
  - Labels: Unbounded.
  - KA: Noto Sans Georgian (wide, bold) or custom lockups.
  - Body: FiraGO throughout.
- **References:** Lite Brite Neon (photographing real neon with restraint), Strava (accent discipline), MUNDIAL (editorial humour), Duolingo (mascot rules).
- **Risk:** this is the closest to the "dark hero + orange accent" cliché of local gyms and to AI-looking neon glow. Choose it only with the restraint rules written down. My ranking for being different is 2 > 1 > 3.

## D. Anti-references (avoid)

- **Orangetheory Fitness:** orange as a corporate franchise colour. Loud, template-like, "burn calories" energy.
- **Barry's, 1Rebel, Gymshark:** red or orange on black, dramatic lighting, "earn it" bro language.
- **Midnight Runners** (https://www.midnightrunners.com, loads): black plus neon party look, slider testimonials, full-bleed hero. Very close to what FITTO must not become.
- **Bloc Shop** (https://blocshop.com, loads): nice tone, but gradient overlays on a dark hero with action photos.
- **Tomo Bouldering Club** (https://www.tomoboulderingclub.com, loads): great copy ("Fun Climbing. real community"), but a generic template layout. The lesson is that tone alone isn't enough without a visual system.
- **Planet Fitness:** cartoon-friendly but cheap. Purple and yellow clip-art, promo-banner style, with the mascot as decoration instead of a system.
- **Liquid Death** (https://liquiddeath.com, loads): a good lesson in putting humour into interface copy, but the dark, edgy, skull style is the opposite of the cozy panda.
- **Webflow and ThemeForest gym templates:** "JOIN NOW" pills, count-up stats, icon grids, trainer carousels with Instagram hover icons.
- **The general patterns:**
  - CSS neon text-shadows on every heading
  - glassmorphism cards
  - purple-to-orange gradients
  - stock photos of shredded models
  - full-bleed photo sliders (the typical Tbilisi gym pattern)
  - bento grids with nothing to organise
